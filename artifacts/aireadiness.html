Artifact Story

Situation

My documentation team began using AI for research, content planning, release analysis, drafting, and knowledge base comparisons. These tools helped us review more information and complete repetitive work faster, but they also introduced serious risks.

AI could turn incomplete source material into confident but inaccurate content, contradict existing documentation without evidence, invent product details, or expose confidential information through unapproved tools. We needed a shared workflow that supported useful AI adoption without treating AI output as verified documentation.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to create a clear AI assisted documentation process that showed writers what they could delegate, which sources they could trust, and where human review remained required. I also needed the process to support writers at different experience levels while keeping each writer responsible for accuracy and quality.

Artifact Story

Action

I organized the workflow around the complete documentation process, from research through publication. I asked writers to gather trusted sources, review existing documentation, and identify missing or conflicting information before using AI.

I defined how writers could use AI to summarize research, compare product changes with existing content, identify documentation gaps, and draft questions for SMEs. I also created different starting points based on experience. New writers could begin with summaries and outlines, while experienced writers could generate a full first draft when reliable source material was available.

I added guardrails that prevented writers from accepting invented procedures, labels, permissions, or product details. I required writers to keep missing information visible as questions, placeholders, or review notes. The writer verified accuracy, a peer reviewed clarity, and the SME confirmed technical behavior.

Artifact Story

Result

I gave writers a faster and more consistent way to use AI without lowering documentation standards. Writers spent less time sorting through sources and creating early drafts, while reviewers and SMEs could focus on questions that required human judgment.

The workflow also reduced the risk of invented details, conflicting language, weak content, and improper handling of confidential information. AI supported the work, but writers remained responsible for everything we published.
