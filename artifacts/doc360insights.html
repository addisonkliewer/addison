Artifact Story

Situation

I supported the SR mobile team through frequent product changes, but I didn't always have a complete picture of how users responded after each release.

Doc360 gave me data about article views, search behavior, failed searches, ratings, feedback, no result queries, and AI search performance. On its own, though, the data didn't explain why users struggled, whether the problem came from documentation or the product, or which gaps mattered most.

I needed to give the SR mobile product manager and lead engineer a clearer view of what users were asking, which content improvements had worked, where coverage was still incomplete, and what questions I couldn't answer without their help.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to turn the available Doc360 analytics into a report that the mobile team could use to make decisions.

My report needed to explain what had changed since the previous release, identify the highest priority documentation gaps, show where users were struggling to find answers, and distinguish between documentation problems, product usability concerns, and questions that required technical validation.

I also needed to surface the information I required from Product and Engineering, including intended behavior, user context, terminology decisions, release timing, ownership, edge cases, and platform limitations.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed Doc360 analytics after each release and organized the findings by analytics category. I examined search volume, article performance, user impact, support frequency, workflow risk, and the size of each content gap.

I built the report around charts, lists of user questions, and recommended actions, focusing especially on permission and access questions, troubleshooting searches, how to questions, feature availability, and setup or configuration needs.

I documented improvements made since the previous release, including new articles, revised content, updated screenshots, reorganized information, expanded troubleshooting guidance, improved release documentation, and stronger search terminology.

I was careful not to treat every analytics pattern as a firm conclusion. I separated confirmed findings from hypotheses, explained where data was incomplete, compared multiple signals, and brought open questions directly to my product manager and lead engineer.

During our working sessions, I presented the analytics, facilitated discussion, asked product and technical questions, recommended priorities, documented decisions, represented the user perspective, and tracked follow up work.

These discussions frequently surfaced information I couldn't see in the analytics alone. I uncovered undocumented edge cases, differences between intended and actual behavior, unclear workflow ownership, usability concerns, product changes that hadn't reached documentation, and gaps between internal assumptions and the questions users were actually asking.

Artifact Story

Result

I gave Juventas and the mobile team a shared view of user needs, documentation performance, and release related content risks.

My findings led to new and revised articles, expanded troubleshooting coverage, updated screenshots, improved release documentation, stronger search terms, and a prioritized documentation backlog. They also supported bug investigation, workflow improvements, product copy changes, feature clarification, better analytics tracking, and earlier documentation involvement in release planning.

Over time, I improved mobile documentation coverage, strengthened relationships between Documentation, Product, and Engineering, and created a repeatable method for evaluating each release.

I learned that documentation analytics are most useful when they lead to better questions. The numbers showed me where to look, while collaboration with Product and Engineering helped me determine what users were experiencing, what the product was intended to do, and which actions would have the greatest impact.
