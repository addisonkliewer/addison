Role Based Learning Path

A structured learning model that organized ScoopRoute training around the responsibilities, permissions, and workflows of five distinct customer personas.

Artifact Brief

Key context for this sample

This artifact shows how I helped transform ScoopRoute's product area training into a role based learning experience. The existing training was organized by product area, which left learners to figure out which courses applied to their responsibilities and in what order to complete them.

I worked with two learning experience designers to validate the personas, map each role to its workflows, review course structure, recommend lesson sequencing, identify content gaps, and revise learning content. The resulting paths connected shared foundational content with the tasks and responsibilities most relevant to each persona.

Artifact Story

Situation

I saw that ScoopRoute customers used the same platform in very different ways depending on their role. Business owners, administrators, route planners, customer service representatives, and delivery staff each needed a different combination of product knowledge.

The existing training organized courses by product area — mobile tools, account management, routing, billing, and reporting — but left learners to decide which content applied to their job and what order made sense.

I recognized this approach treated different users too similarly. It also made it harder to scale customer onboarding, support continued product adoption, and help learners move beyond isolated features toward complete job workflows.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to help two learning experience designers create learning paths that reflected how customers actually used ScoopRoute.

My role included validating the personas and their workflows, reviewing the course structure, recommending lesson sequences, identifying documentation and training gaps, and editing learning content.

I wanted the paths to show each learner what to complete, what knowledge their role required, and how individual product features connected to their daily responsibilities — while reducing dependence on instructor led onboarding and helping existing customers adopt more of the platform.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed help center documentation, existing courses, and subject matter expert guidance to understand the workflows tied to each persona, then helped identify five primary personas and map their daily responsibilities, product permissions, support needs, and onboarding expectations. Instead of assigning only unique tasks to each role, we mapped a combination of core workflows and role specific responsibilities.

I linked shared courses across multiple learning paths rather than recreating them for each persona, then organized each path around role responsibilities — starting with basic navigation and moving into advanced workflows, less frequent tasks, and administrative responsibilities.

I worked closely with the learning experience designers through planning sessions, content mapping workshops, shared documents, comments, direct editing, asynchronous reviews, and informal discussions. They led the learning architecture, objectives, assessments, video recommendations, and interactive design, while I contributed product and documentation context, validated the workflow mapping, and helped make sure the course sequence matched how customers used the platform. I built the paths around knowledge checks, hands on exercises, product simulations, and final assessments, drawing on existing documentation for approved source content, deeper reference material, shared terminology, and coverage for topics that didn't need a full lesson.

Through the review process, I uncovered several documentation gaps and areas where existing lessons needed revision, so I added missing content, changed lesson sequences, and clarified role descriptions where the original structure didn't accurately reflect the learner journey.

Artifact Story

Result

I gave customers a clearer way to navigate ScoopRoute training according to their responsibilities.

Learners received more focused onboarding, clearer academy navigation, and a stronger connection between training and the tasks they were expected to perform. I also created a prioritized roadmap for missing content, improved reuse of existing courses, and identified where documentation or training needed additional coverage.

Over time, my model supported more scalable onboarding and more consistent role based training, and created a reusable framework that could be adapted for future personas without rebuilding the learning system from the beginning.

This project showed me how persona analysis, workflow mapping, information architecture, documentation strategy, and instructional design can work together to make training more practical and easier to maintain.
