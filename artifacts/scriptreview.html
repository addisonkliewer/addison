Artifact Story

Situation

The learning team was preparing a recorded product walkthrough — showing customers how to update customer information within a mobile workflow — for an upcoming training deadline. A learning experience designer had completed the first script draft, but the narration still needed a technical review before recording.

Even a polished script could create problems if it described controls that no longer appeared in the interface, presented screens in the wrong order, used terminology that differed from the documentation, or gave the narrator more information than could fit alongside the screen activity. Because walkthroughs teach by showing and explaining an action at the same time, accuracy and timing had to work together.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to review the script for technical accuracy, editorial clarity, and recording readiness — confirming that each narrated step matched the current interface, checking the workflow sequence, aligning terminology with the help center, removing unnecessary information, and making sure the spoken instructions could keep pace with the screen recording.

After production began, I also needed to review selected sections of the draft video and identify issues that weren't obvious from the script alone, including awkward transitions, screen timing, and missing visual actions.

Artifact Story

Action

I followed the script in a ScoopRoute test environment, comparing each instruction against current product behavior, and checked it against the help documentation. I found minor inconsistencies involving screen order, state dependent behavior, and missing interface context, along with terminology that didn't consistently match the help center — I revised those lines so learners would hear the same terms they saw in the product. The script needed light copyediting rather than a major rewrite: I trimmed unnecessary background information, added missing troubleshooting context and role specific guidance, and broke narration into smaller segments with natural pause points to give the recording room to complete each visible action.

I delivered feedback through tracked changes, comments, and a written summary across one or two review rounds before recording. Once production began, I reviewed selected sections of the draft video and caught awkward pauses, transitions, and missing screen actions that hadn't been apparent on the page, which led to a small follow up revision round to correct the narration and clarify the visual sequence.

Artifact Story

Result

The final walkthrough more closely matched the ScoopRoute product, used terminology consistent with the documentation, and gave the production team clearer instructions for coordinating narration with screen activity. The review prevented outdated or incomplete interface guidance from reaching learners, improved narration clarity, and helped the producer identify which portions needed adjustment before publication.

Although the project didn't include a formal learner performance metric, it produced concrete improvements: fewer corrections were needed after the initial review, the recording required less extensive revision, approval moved more efficiently, and the producer received clearer guidance for the final cut. The work also gave me direct experience with e-learning script development, evaluating every spoken instruction against what the learner would actually see and do.
