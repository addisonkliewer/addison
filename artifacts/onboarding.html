Artifact Story

Situation

New technical writers needed to learn a complex product, several documentation systems, established publishing workflows, and a large stakeholder network. Important knowledge lived across internal pages, team conversations, existing documentation, and senior writers’ experience. Without a central resource, new writers received inconsistent guidance, asked repeated questions, and relied heavily on whoever was available to help.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to make onboarding more consistent and easier to navigate. The guide had to explain what writers should learn first, where to find key resources, how documentation moved from planning through publication, and who owned each product area. It also needed to support independent learning without replacing mentorship, observation, and direct questions.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed and improved the onboarding guide while mentoring new writers who used it. I organized the resource as an internal wiki page and checklist covering team structure, product areas, user roles, terminology, writing standards, reviews, publishing, stakeholder relationships, system access, and tools such as Doc360, Jira, Confluence, Slack, analytics platforms, and testing environments.

I separated immediate tasks from topics writers could learn later and used short checklists, milestones, examples, and links to deeper resources. I also encouraged writers to learn through shadowing, product demonstrations, SME meetings, support issue reviews, and existing documentation. Feedback from new writers helped us clarify responsibilities, improve instructions, and add missing resources.

Artifact Story

Result

My guide improvements created a more consistent onboarding experience and gave new writers clearer expectations from the beginning.

Our new team members had easier access to product knowledge, workflows, team standards, and key resources, and managers and experienced writers had a shared reference they could use during onboarding instead of rebuilding the process for each new hire.

The improved guide reduced repeated explanations, supported stronger writer confidence, and made team knowledge easier to maintain as people, products, and processes changed.

It also demonstrated a broader leadership approach I believe in: onboarding works best when clear documentation, thoughtful process design, direct mentorship, and long term governance support one another.
