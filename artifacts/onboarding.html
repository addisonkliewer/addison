Writer Onboarding Guide

A structured onboarding resource that helped new technical writers learn the team, product, tools, workflows, and expectations required to contribute successfully.

Artifact Brief

Key context for this sample

This artifact demonstrates how I supported new writers by turning scattered team knowledge into a practical onboarding resource. The guide was created for new technical writers joining ScoopRoute. It brought together information that had previously lived across internal systems, team conversations, individual experience, and undocumented working norms. The resource covered team responsibilities, product learning, tools, writing workflows, review processes, publishing expectations, terminology, and stakeholder relationships. It also gave new writers a central place to find essential information while leaving room for direct mentorship and questions during onboarding.

Artifact Story

Situation

New technical writers needed to learn a complex product, several documentation systems, established publishing workflows, and a wide network of stakeholders.

Important knowledge was spread across internal pages, existing documentation, team conversations, and the experience of senior writers. Some expectations were documented, while others existed as team habits or informal knowledge.

Without a central onboarding resource, new writers could receive different guidance depending on who was available to help. This created repeated questions, unclear expectations, and a heavier mentoring burden for experienced team members.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to help make onboarding more consistent and easier to navigate.

The guide needed to explain what new writers should learn first, where to find important resources, how documentation work moved from planning through publication, and who owned different parts of the product.

It also needed to support independent learning without replacing direct mentorship. New writers still needed space to ask questions, observe experienced teammates, meet subject matter experts, and learn how the team worked in practice.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed and edited the guide while also serving as a mentor to new writers using it.

The resource was organized as an internal wiki page and checklist. It covered team structure, product areas, user roles, major workflows, terminology, writing standards, review expectations, publishing processes, stakeholder relationships, and system access.

The guide introduced writers to tools such as Doc360, Jira, Confluence, Slack, product testing environments, analytics systems, and collaboration tools. It also explained how writers planned work, drafted content, coordinated reviews, published updates, and maintained documentation over time.

To make the information manageable, I separated immediate onboarding tasks from topics writers could learn later. I used short checklists, clear milestones, examples, and links to deeper resources rather than placing every detail on one page.

The onboarding process also encouraged writers to learn through shadowing, product demonstrations, meetings with subject matter experts, support issue reviews, and reading existing documentation. I remained available for direct questions so the guide functioned as a starting point rather than a substitute for human support.

Feedback from new writers helped us clarify responsibilities, improve instructions, add missing resources, and align the guide more closely with the way the team actually worked.

Artifact Story

Result

The guide created a more consistent onboarding experience and gave new writers clearer expectations from the beginning.

New team members had easier access to product knowledge, workflows, team standards, and key resources. Managers and experienced writers also had a shared reference they could use during onboarding instead of rebuilding the process for each new hire.

The guide reduced repeated explanations, supported stronger writer confidence, and made team knowledge easier to maintain as people, products, and processes changed.

It also demonstrated a broader leadership approach: onboarding works best when clear documentation, thoughtful process design, direct mentorship, and long term governance support one another.
