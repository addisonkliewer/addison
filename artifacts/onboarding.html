Artifact Story

Situation

I saw that new technical writers needed to learn a complex product, several documentation systems, established publishing workflows, and a wide network of stakeholders.

Important knowledge was spread across internal pages, existing documentation, team conversations, and the experience of senior writers, and I found some expectations were documented while others existed only as team habits or informal knowledge.

Without a central onboarding resource, new writers could receive different guidance depending on who was available to help, which created repeated questions, unclear expectations, and a heavier mentoring burden for experienced team members.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to help make onboarding more consistent and easier to navigate.

I wanted the guide to explain what new writers should learn first, where to find important resources, how documentation work moved from planning through publication, and who owned different parts of the product.

I also wanted it to support independent learning without replacing direct mentorship — new writers still needed space to ask questions, observe experienced teammates, meet subject matter experts, and learn how the team worked in practice.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed and edited the existing guide while also serving as a mentor to new writers using it. I organized the resource as an internal wiki page and checklist covering team structure, product areas, user roles, major workflows, terminology, writing standards, review expectations, publishing processes, stakeholder relationships, and system access.

I helped introduce writers to tools such as Doc360, Jira, Confluence, Slack, product testing environments, analytics systems, and collaboration tools, and explained how writers planned work, drafted content, coordinated reviews, published updates, and maintained documentation over time.

To make the information manageable, I separated immediate onboarding tasks from topics writers could learn later, using short checklists, clear milestones, examples, and links to deeper resources rather than placing every detail on one page. I also encouraged writers to learn through shadowing, product demonstrations, meetings with subject matter experts, support issue reviews, and reading existing documentation, and stayed available for direct questions so the guide functioned as a starting point rather than a substitute for human support.

Feedback from our new writers helped me clarify responsibilities, improve instructions, add missing resources, and align the guide more closely with the way the team actually worked.

Artifact Story

Result

My guide improvements created a more consistent onboarding experience and gave new writers clearer expectations from the beginning.

Our new team members had easier access to product knowledge, workflows, team standards, and key resources, and managers and experienced writers had a shared reference they could use during onboarding instead of rebuilding the process for each new hire.

The improved guide reduced repeated explanations, supported stronger writer confidence, and made team knowledge easier to maintain as people, products, and processes changed.

It also demonstrated a broader leadership approach I believe in: onboarding works best when clear documentation, thoughtful process design, direct mentorship, and long term governance support one another.
