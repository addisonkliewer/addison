Docs to Training Transformation

A transformation of reference focused help content into a role based learning experience that guides new customers through customer account management workflows.

Artifact Brief

Key context for this sample

I helped turn my existing help center documentation into structured training for new customers. My source material was accurate, but I had written it for users who needed to look up individual answers — spread across multiple articles, weighed down with advanced details new learners didn't yet need, and missing the sequence, context, and practice required for training.

Working as a supporting technical writer, reviewer, editor, and project team member, I helped reshape my documentation with the LX team into a self paced academy experience with video and walkthrough elements, while preserving the terminology and approved guidance used in the help center.

Artifact Story

Situation

We saw that new customers needed an Academy on using ScoopRoutes, but most of the available source material existed as my help center articles. My articles worked well when someone already knew what they needed to find, but they didn't provide a clear path for learning the workflow from beginning to end.

We found the content spread across several pages, assuming some existing product knowledge, including details better suited for later reference, and missing practice, assessment, or role specific guidance. I needed to help the LX team and customer-facing teams scale onboarding, close a gap in creating the academy, and reduce the repeated questions reaching customer facing teams.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to support the transformation of accurate reference documentation into a more focused learning experience for new customers.

We needed the training to prioritize common tasks, align with learner goals, and retain the information that subject matter experts and trainers considered essential. We also needed it to introduce concepts in a logical order, separate role specific guidance where necessary, and give learners ways to check their understanding rather than simply read through product instructions.

Artifact Story

Action

I reviewed the published help articles and worked with instructional designers, customer success teams, support teams, and academy leadership to identify which information belonged in the course.

I prioritized common customer tasks, learner goals, and expert recommendations. I removed repeated explanations, rare edge cases, and advanced workflows that learners could return to later in the help center.

I added learning objectives, knowledge checks, role specific context, and demonstration guidance that hadn't existed in the original articles. I organized the material by user role first, then arranged the lessons around the sequence in which learners would complete the work.

As a supporting technical writer and editor, I reviewed the training for accuracy, clarity, terminology, and alignment with the existing documentation. I also revised the sequence, shortened lessons, reduced unnecessary technical detail, separated content for different roles, and improved assessments during review.

Artifact Story

Result

We created a more consistent and approachable onboarding experience while preserving the accuracy of the original documentation.

We gave learners a guided progression instead of a collection of disconnected reference articles. The training supported better comprehension, stronger onboarding, and fewer repeated questions, while the documentation remained available for deeper reference after the course.

We also improved alignment between academy content and the help center. Approved terminology and source content could be reused across both systems, making future learning materials easier to develop and maintain.
