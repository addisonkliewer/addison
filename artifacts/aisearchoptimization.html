Artifact Detail

AI Search Optimization

An analysis and content improvement project that used AI search data to identify documentation gaps, strengthen article structure, and improve the quality of answers returned to users.

Artifact Brief

Key context for this sample

This artifact demonstrates how I treated AI search performance as a content quality problem rather than only a search technology problem. Working with Rhea, I reviewed Doc360 analytics to understand which questions the AI search experience answered successfully and where users received incomplete, irrelevant, or unsuccessful responses. We focused especially on troubleshooting questions, procedural how to searches, and queries related to product releases. The findings showed that AI search quality depended heavily on the documentation underneath it. Missing content, inconsistent terminology, unclear titles, weak information architecture, and poorly structured articles all limited the system's ability to retrieve and assemble useful answers. I used those insights to recommend and implement improvements across individual articles and the wider content system.

Artifact Story

Situation

ScoopRoute's AI search experience depended on a large documentation set containing procedural guidance, troubleshooting information, release updates, and product concepts.

The search tool could retrieve and summarize existing information, but it could not compensate for missing articles, inconsistent terminology, vague titles, or content that lacked a clear structure. When users searched for troubleshooting help or asked how to complete a workflow, an unsuccessful answer often pointed to a deeper documentation problem rather than a limitation of the AI alone.

The team needed a more systematic way to understand why searches failed and which content improvements would have the greatest effect.

Artifact Story

Task

I needed to help evaluate AI search performance and translate the findings into practical documentation improvements.

The work required more than identifying unsuccessful searches. I needed to determine whether each issue came from a missing article, inconsistent product language, weak information architecture, insufficient metadata, an unclear title, or content that did not state the answer directly enough for reliable retrieval.

The goal was to improve the search experience while also strengthening the documentation for human readers, future maintenance, and reuse across other AI supported workflows.

Artifact Story

Action

I collaborated with Rhea to review Doc360 search analytics and evaluate the questions users were asking. We focused on troubleshooting, procedural how to, and release related queries because these searches often reflected an immediate user need and exposed clear gaps in the content system.

I grouped unsuccessful and low quality responses by their likely cause. Some searches revealed that the necessary documentation did not exist. Others showed that the information was present but difficult to retrieve because articles used inconsistent terminology, unclear titles, weak taxonomy, limited metadata, or structures that buried the answer.

I used the findings to prioritize new documentation and recommend revisions to existing content. These improvements included creating missing articles, aligning terminology with the language users searched for, strengthening titles and headings, making procedures easier to scan, adding clearer context, and organizing related information more consistently.

Rather than optimizing content only for an AI tool, I focused on changes that improved both machine retrieval and the human reading experience.

Artifact Story

Result

The project contributed to an approximately 50 percent improvement in successful AI search responses, measured through the search success rate in Doc360.

The strongest gains came from creating documentation that had previously been missing and improving the structure of existing articles. Better titles, terminology, metadata, taxonomy, and information architecture gave the AI more reliable source material while also making the knowledge base easier for customers and internal teams to navigate directly.

The work reinforced an important lesson: AI search quality is built on documentation quality. When content is accurate, clearly named, consistently structured, and connected through useful metadata, it becomes stronger infrastructure for search, support, product education, and future AI assisted content workflows.
